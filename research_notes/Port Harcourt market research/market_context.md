# Port Harcourt market context for Cosentino A–F analysis

**Cut-off:** 2 October 2026. **Geography:** Port Harcourt metropolitan market and its immediate project/logistics catchment (Obio/Akpor, Woji, Trans-Amadi, Eleme and Onne where relevant). **Reference distributor:** Stone Depot by Impacto, Lekki, Lagos.

## How to read this memo

- **Verified/public fact:** stated by an official source, a company’s own current website, or a named directory listing. Company self-descriptions are still self-reported and are marked where that matters.
- **Triangulated signal:** repeated across more than one public source, but not a complete market census.
- **Commercial assumption:** a planning recommendation or range for Cosentino; it is not a sourced market size, price or freight quote.
- **Negative evidence:** no public evidence found in the searches listed in the source log. It does not prove that a brand or capability does not exist privately.

## Executive judgement

Port Harcourt is a credible **premium, project-led expansion market**, with a directional Cosentino potential score of **7/10**. The opportunity is concentrated in oil-and-gas-linked executive housing, upscale new-build estates, hospitality, corporate interiors and the Onne/Trans-Amadi industrial ecosystem. It is not appropriate to present the city as a measured mass-market opportunity: I found no reliable public dataset for the Port Harcourt premium-surface market, number of fabricators, annual slab consumption or total premium-project count.

The recommended model is a **hybrid**:

1. Keep product ownership, importing, technical governance and complex fabrication with Stone Depot by Impacto in Lekki.
2. Add a Port Harcourt-based preferred fabricator/showroom or small sample-and-service point after a field audit, rather than granting a full sub-distributorship immediately.
3. Sell through direct project specification to developers, architects, interior studios, contractors and oil-and-gas/hospitality operators, while the local partner handles measurement, fabrication and installation.
4. Use a local warehouse/buffer only after repeat demand is proven; do not tie up cash in a broad slab range at launch.

The central commercial constraint is not absence of demand. It is the combination of **long road supply from Lagos, fragile-slab handling, local traffic/security friction, currency and material-price volatility, and uncertain public evidence of advanced fabrication capacity**.

# A. Market potential

## A1. Population and economic base

There is no current official 2026 population census for Port Harcourt city in the public sources reviewed. The most useful public planning signal is a 2024 BusinessDay feature that cites 2022 projections of **774,600 for Port Harcourt City LGA and 665,000 for Obio/Akpor**, or approximately **1.44 million across those two core urban LGAs**; the same article cites **7.4768 million for Rivers State in 2022**. These are projections, not a new census and not a precise metropolitan boundary. [BusinessDay population and Ring Road feature](https://cdn.businessday.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/BD_20240414.pdf)

The older National Bureau of Statistics/National Population Commission forecast table gives **6,375,176 for Rivers State in 2016**, illustrating why a single “current Port Harcourt population” number should not be used without stating the source and boundary. [NBS/Population Commission forecasts](https://nigerianstat.gov.ng/pdfuploads/Population%20Forecasts.pdf)

Rivers is economically significant within Nigeria. NBS reports **₦195.41 billion of internally generated revenue in 2023**, one of the three highest state totals after Lagos and the FCT. IGR is not household wealth or GDP, but it is a useful signal of formal economic activity and taxable corporate/professional employment. [NBS IGR 2023](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/elibrary/read/1241579)

The Rivers investment-promotion narrative is directionally consistent with the commercial evidence: oil and gas, maritime, manufacturing, real estate and infrastructure are all presented as investment sectors. However, the Rivers investment pages include headline estimates such as state GDP and population without a fully transparent underlying calculation; I use them as directional context, not as hard market-size inputs. [NIPC Rivers profile](https://nipc.gov.ng/nigeria/rivers); [Rivers State Investment Promotion Agency sectors](https://rsipa.riversstateapps.ng/sectors/)

## A2. Oil, gas, downstream and maritime demand signals

Port Harcourt remains an important operating and services centre for the Niger Delta petroleum economy, even though the commercial footprint is changing. NUPRC’s 2024 annual report attributes **50.84 million barrels of crude/condensate and 391.31 million Mscf of gas to Rivers** in its state-basis table for the covered period (November 2023–September 2024). This is production attributed to the state, not Port Harcourt city, but it supports the existence of a substantial regional energy ecosystem. [NUPRC 2024 Annual Report](https://www.nuprc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/UPDATED-NUPRC-2024-ANNUAL-REPORT-1.pdf)

The Onne Oil & Gas Free Zone is the strongest nearby industrial signal. OGFZA states that the zone has licensed **more than 200 companies**, represents about **$20 billion of investment**, and includes eight principal oil companies and more than 220 service companies across its FOT/FLT terminals. These are official zone claims and should not be converted into an addressable surface-volume estimate, but they support direct specification opportunities in offices, accommodation, hospitality and support facilities. [OGFZA Onne](https://ogfza.gov.ng/free-zones/onne/)

The Nigerian Ports Authority describes Onne as a multi-purpose cargo port handling general, bulk, oil-well equipment and containerised cargo, with access to West African and Sub-Saharan oil fields. It reports that Onne accounts for over 65% of export cargo through Nigerian seaports; that figure is an NPA claim and should be treated as a port-level positioning signal, not a forecast for Cosentino. [NPA Onne Port Complex](https://nigerianports.gov.ng/onne/)

The refinery signal is positive for industrial relevance but **not a dependable current demand forecast**. NNPC announced a 60,000 bpd old-refinery restart at 70% capacity in November 2024, while NMDPRA’s 2025 fact sheet records that PHRC was shut down on 24 May 2025 for planned maintenance and sustainability assessment. In May 2026 NNPC announced a memorandum of understanding with Chinese companies aimed at restarting/expanding the Port Harcourt and Warri refineries; it was a potential technical-equity partnership, not proof of completed restart. [NNPC November 2024 statement](https://www.nnpcgroup.com/insights/nnpc-ltd-reaffirms-progress-on-port-harcourt-refinery-and-addresses-misleading-claims); [NMDPRA 2025 fact sheet](https://alps.blob.core.windows.net/nmdprawebsite/Statistics/Upload-80c82709-7f84-4cea-959e-668d6e6d030e.pdf); [NNPC May 2026 MoU](https://www.nnpcgroup.com/insights/press-release-nnpc-chinese-firms-sign-mo-u-towards-restart-expansion-port-harcourt-refineries)

There is also a counter-signal: BusinessDay reported in August 2025 that oil-company divestment, offshore migration, political conflict and youth unrest had weakened some city activity, while also reporting that new operators and estates could support a recovery. The commercial implication is to diversify beyond IOC head offices: target service companies, indigenous operators, contractors, developers, hospitality and executive housing. [BusinessDay, “Port Harcourt city in declining mode?”](https://businessday.ng/life/article/port-harcourt-city-in-declining-mode/)

## A4. Demand score and interpretation

**Directional Cosentino premium-surface potential: 7/10, medium-low confidence.**

- **Upside:** energy-sector purchasing power, corporate/executive housing, visible estate development, a strong hospitality/office base, Onne logistics and a meaningful local design/contractor community.
- **Constraint:** premium demand is narrow and cyclical; many projects are private and opaque; natural granite/marble and imported tiles are entrenched; inflation/FX creates quote volatility; transport and installation failures can erase margin.
- **No reliable public market size:** do not publish an annual ₦ or m² market estimate until Stone Depot and a local partner share 12 months of quotes, wins/losses, project sizes and competitor price checks.

# B. Fabrication ecosystem and clusters

## B1. Where to concentrate field work

**GRA / Old GRA / New GRA.** This is the premium residential and executive-housing cluster. A property-market guide describes GRA as the city’s top residential address and New GRA/Woji as newer managed-estate areas serving oil-industry professionals. Use the guide for cluster orientation, not as a transaction database. [Port Harcourt neighbourhood guide](https://propertyaccess.ng/complete-guide-of-port-harcourt/)

**Peter Odili Road / Trans-Woji / Woji.** This is the strongest premium-development corridor: Sunville, Bauhaus, Cribz, Temamost and other public projects repeatedly place developments here. It combines luxury residential, hospitality, restaurants, showrooms and access to Trans-Amadi.

**Trans-Amadi / Rumuola / Aba Road / 1st Artillery.** This is the industrial, contractor, oil-service, materials and logistics cluster. Public sources identify Trans-Amadi as an industrial area and list stone/material dealers around Aba Road, Rumuigbo, NTA Road, Mile 3 and Mgbuosimiri. It is the logical location for a service partner or secure buffer warehouse, but not necessarily the best address for a luxury consumer showroom.

**Rumuigbo / Mgbuoba / Ozuoba / Mile 3 / Ikwerre Road.** Public directory listings show several marble/granite dealers in these corridors. Treat them as a lead list requiring physical verification; the directories do not establish turnover, machinery or current activity.

**Eleme / Onne.** This is an industrial catchment rather than a city-centre retail cluster. It matters for oil-and-gas offices, camps, hospitality, port facilities, contractors and project logistics. It should be covered in project-specification visits, not as the first retail showroom location.

## D2. Competitive implications

- **Natural stone wins on local familiarity, one-off availability and price flexibility.** The likely weakness is inconsistent brand/technical support and variable fabrication quality; this is a commercial inference to validate with quote comparisons.
- **Caesarstone is the most credible directly branded rival in public evidence.** It may offer a useful counterpoint: a local showroom/fabricator promise close to the customer, while Cosentino can differentiate with Dekton applications, project support, breadth and Stone Depot’s existing fabrication base.
- **Imported tiles/porcelain compete for visual budgets but are not identical to a high-performance slab system.** The sales message should compare application, templating, joints, installation, durability, warranty and total installed cost—not only slab price.
- **The market may be less crowded by named international slab brands than Lagos, but this is not a verified market share claim.** It creates an opening for specification leadership, not permission to assume no competitor exists.

# E. Logistics: Stone Depot (Lekki, Lagos) to Port Harcourt

## E1. Route, distance and time

The public route is the Lagos–Ibadan/Sagamu–Benin/Ore corridor, continuing through the southern route and East–West Road into Port Harcourt. One route page gives **606 km and 7 h 35 min** between Lagos and Port Harcourt; another gives **618 km and about 8 h 10 min**; Travelmath gives **8 h 37 min**. The differences reflect endpoints and routing. [AFRoute](https://afroute.com/ng/lagos/port-harcourt); [DistancesFrom](https://www.distancesfrom.com/ng/directions-from-Lagos-to-Port-Harcourt/DirectionHistory/249619.aspx); [Travelmath](https://www.travelmath.com/driving-time/from/Lagos,+Nigeria/to/Port+Harcourt,+Nigeria)

Stone Depot is in Lekki Peninsula II, not Lagos city centre. A practical planning range is therefore **approximately 620–650 km from the Stone Depot address to a Port Harcourt delivery point**, but this should be confirmed on each carrier quote. That Lekki-specific range is a planning estimate, not a sourced route measurement.

Public carrier pages show a meaningful difference between “driving time” and **door-to-door freight time**:

- GoFlow publishes Lagos–Port Harcourt full-load haulage at **2–3 days** and says nationwide deliveries are typically 3–5 days. [GoFlow freight](https://goflow.com.ng/freight)
- Travo advertises approximately 610 km and **1–2 days** for Lagos–Port Harcourt haulage, with flatbeds, container trucks, insurance and fragile/high-value freight options. [Travo haulage](https://services.travo.ng/haulage-services/)
- HaulConnect says common interstate routes including Lagos–Port Harcourt are often **24–48 hours**, dependent on distance and cargo. [HaulConnect](https://www.haulconnect.com.ng/)

For Cosentino slabs, plan **2–3 working days door-to-door in normal conditions, 3–5 days in a disruption window**, plus loading/inspection time in Lekki and local last-mile scheduling. Do not promise the 7–9-hour car time to a project client.

## E2. Road risk and local last mile

The route is operational but volatile. In February 2026, reporting described severe gridlock on the Lagos–Ibadan and Sagamu–Benin highways during rehabilitation works, with queues lasting hours. [Vanguard traffic report](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/02/motorists-decry-gridlock-on-lagos-ibadan-sagamu-benin-highways/amp/)

Inside the destination market, an academic study identifies the Eleme Junction–Refinery–Onne, NPA–Mile Interchange, Tank Interchange–Rumuokoro, Trans-Amadi–Garrison, Mile 1–Agip–Mile 3 and Rumuola–Aba Road–Oyigbo corridors as major freight routes. It reports congestion, safety/security, poor-road, harassment and multiple-taxation barriers. Use the study as a risk flag rather than a current service-level guarantee. [Port Harcourt freight-corridor study](https://doi.org/10.15580/gjss.2025.1.032425044)

Operational controls for slabs:

- Load in a dedicated, covered/flatbed vehicle suitable for A-frames; keep slabs vertical, padded, strapped and photographed at origin.
- Avoid mixed loads that can shift against crates. Use goods-in-transit insurance that explicitly covers breakage, not only total loss.
- Dispatch early in the week and during daylight; avoid holiday/peak-traffic windows where possible.
- Require GPS tracking, driver/vehicle details, proof of delivery, and a named receiving contact.
- Build in a local 24-hour receiving/inspection window before installation.
- Use two pre-qualified carriers; never make a single carrier the only service option.

## E3. Freight modes and cost evidence

**Road full truckload is the default.** Public corridor platforms list flatbeds/platform trailers, curtainsider/box trucks, tractor-trailers and 20/40-ft container trucks for Lagos–Port Harcourt and construction/industrial cargo. [BtSh corridor](https://btsh.africa/corridor/lagos-portharcourt)

**Groupage/parcel is suitable for samples and small accessories, not full slabs.** It may be cheaper, but introduces extra handling and breakage points. Use it only for A4 samples, hardware, documents or small mock-up pieces.

**Sea/port routing is a future import option, not a substitute for the current Lagos-to-PH transfer.** Onne has container/general cargo capability, and Hapag-Lloyd advertises inland delivery from Lagos ports to Port Harcourt and Onne. This matters if Cosentino later imports directly or uses Onne for project cargo; it is not evidence that Stone Depot stock can be economically repositioned by water. [Hapag-Lloyd Inland Nigeria](https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/content/dam/website/downloads/pdf/24380_Flyer_Inland_Nigeria_Updated.pdf)

Public freight pricing is thin. Flagship publishes a Lagos–Port Harcourt lane at **200 units**, defines a base unit of **₦10,000**, and shows a diesel/commission methodology. That implies a nominal ₦2m lane benchmark under that site’s formula, but it is not a slab-specific written quote and the site explicitly links the unit to diesel and load date. [Flagship lane/rate methodology](https://www.flagshipnigeria.com/)

**Planning estimate, not market fact:** budget **₦2–4m per dedicated Lagos–Port Harcourt slab movement**, excluding fabrication, installation, customs and taxes. This range is deliberately provisional: it takes the public ₦2m lane benchmark as an anchor and allows for slab-compatible equipment, loading/handling, insurance, city last mile, tolls and peak-road risk. Before launch, obtain three written quotes for the actual Stone Depot address, slab weight/number of crates, vehicle type, insurance and delivery point.

## E4. Warehousing and last mile

Potential operating options:

- **Stone Depot/Lekki dispatch:** simplest at launch; no local inventory, full control and minimal channel conflict.
- **Trans-Amadi third-party warehouse:** Trans Amadi Logistics advertises a Port Harcourt facility with 24/7 security, climate-control options and inventory management, plus flatbeds and bulk-cargo movement. This is a vendor lead; verify legal status, insurance, slab A-frame storage and actual capacity before using it. [Trans Amadi Logistics](https://www.transamadilogistics.com/)
- **Quicka Nigeria:** Port Harcourt-headquartered freight/project-logistics provider with a Lagos/Abuja network, publicly claiming 28,000+ m² of open/covered/bonded storage and oil-and-gas/construction coverage. Treat capacity as self-reported until audited. [Quicka](https://quickaltd.com/)
- **Onne/OGFZA:** official port/free-zone infrastructure includes significant warehouse and industrial areas, suited to project cargo and oil-and-gas-related inventory. It is likely too remote/industrial for a consumer showroom but useful for larger contract logistics. [OGFZA Onne](https://ogfza.gov.ng/free-zones/onne/); [NPA Onne](https://nigerianports.gov.ng/onne/)

Recommended launch configuration: **no permanent slab warehouse; one controlled sample/display kit in Port Harcourt; direct full-slab dispatch from Lekki; local partner receives/installs.** Add a small secure buffer only when repeat orders justify the handling cost.

# F. Practical market-entry plan

## F1. Model decision

| Model | Assessment | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Direct from Stone Depot to every client | Lowest fixed cost and best product control; slower local response and weaker local trust | Use for launch and large/complex jobs |
| Local sub-distributor | Improves reach and stock access; creates price/channel conflict and demands working capital | Do not grant at launch; consider after a 6-month evidence gate |
| Local fabricator/showroom | Best local customer experience and installation response; requires technical audit and training | Preferred phase-2 model |
| Direct project specification | Highest strategic value and avoids premature inventory; needs regular visits and samples | Start immediately |
| Hybrid | Stone Depot owns product/technical standards; local partner owns local response; direct spec drives demand | **Recommended** |

The first Port Harcourt partner should be a **fabricator/showroom hybrid**, preferably in or near the Trans-Amadi/Peter Odili interface, with a luxury-facing sample presence that can reach GRA/Woji/New GRA. It should be non-exclusive initially. Exclusivity should require minimum quarterly volume, trained installers, price discipline, lead registration, warranty compliance and no undisclosed competing-brand conflicts.

## F2. First-year measurable priorities (planning targets)

These are commercial targets, not forecasts:

- Map **50–60 named accounts**: 15 fabricators/material dealers, 15 interior/architecture studios, 10 developers/contractors, 5 hotel/hospitality operators and 5 oil-and-gas/Onne facility or EPC contacts.
- Complete **30 first meetings**, **12 technical/sample presentations**, **8 qualified project opportunities**, and **4 audited fabricator/installer candidates**.
- Secure **2–4 reference projects** in GRA/Woji/Peter Odili/Trans-Amadi, with photography and permission to use the work in specification selling.
- Achieve a pilot target of **250–400 m² cumulative paid volume in 12 months**, with **600–800 m² as a stretch target** if two developer/hospitality wins land. These are deliberately modest hypotheses because the market size is unknown.
- Initial product-mix hypothesis: **50% Silestone/Sensa countertop and vanity work, 35% Dekton for kitchens, hospitality, outdoor/facade and high-performance applications, 15% natural stone/other Cosentino categories**. Rebase after the first ten quotes.
- Service KPIs: quote within 72 hours after a complete brief, route ETA variance below one working day in normal periods, damage/claim rate below 3% of shipped slabs, and at least two trained local installers able to pass a test installation.

## F3. Visit cadence: October 2026–September 2027

**Launch trip: five working days, two people.**

- Day 1: Stone Depot operating review, price/lead-time/packaging baseline, competitor sample review.
- Day 2: Old GRA/New GRA and luxury showrooms; meet interior studios and furniture/lifestyle specifiers.
- Day 3: Peter Odili Road, Trans-Woji and Woji; visit developer sites and contractors.
- Day 4: Trans-Amadi, Aba Road, Rumuigbo, Mgbuoba/Mile 3; audit fabricators and materials channels.
- Day 5: Eleme/Onne project/logistics meetings, carrier/warehouse checks and partner scorecard.

Then make **six to eight in-person trips per year**, roughly every 6–8 weeks during the pilot, with monthly remote pipeline reviews. Alternate the emphasis: one visit for specification/developers, the next for fabrication/installation/quality. A local partner should report weekly on quotes, sample requests, site measurements, wins/losses and competitor prices.

## F4. Budget envelope

The following is a **planning envelope in October 2026 naira, not a sourced quote**. Assumptions: two travelling staff, six to eight trips, three-to-five nights per trip, local driver/security arrangements, two technical events, sample/display support and two pilot deliveries; it excludes slab inventory, customs and major showroom construction.

- Travel, lodging, local transport and field security: **₦6–12m**.
- Samples, display boards, technical materials and partner training: **₦3–7m**.
- Pilot freight, insurance, test cuts and site-support contingency: **₦4–10m**.
- Total first-year activation envelope before inventory: **₦15–30m**, with a 20–30% contingency inside or on top of the envelope depending on Cosentino’s risk policy.
- Local sample/display point or partner fit-out: add a separately approved **₦3–8m planning allowance** only after the partner passes the audit; get vendor quotes before committing.

The biggest budget uncertainty is not travel—it is how much working capital the partner needs for slabs, crates, machinery, power backup, insurance and local installation. Keep that decision gated by actual run-rate.

## F5. Stage gates

**Gate 1 (0–90 days):** no exclusivity; validate 50–60 accounts, obtain three freight quotes, audit four workshops, run one technical session, and secure the first qualified project briefs.

**Gate 2 (90–180 days):** choose one preferred local fabricator/showroom if it passes equipment, HSE, quality and commercial checks; place controlled displays; close at least one paid reference project; measure real delivery time and damage rate.

**Gate 3 (180–365 days):** consider a small local buffer or formal sub-distributor only if there are at least three consecutive months of repeat demand, a defensible project pipeline, an acceptable damage/claim rate and a partner willing to meet minimum purchase/service commitments.

## F6. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Road rehabilitation, traffic and local freight congestion | Slab delay, breakage, missed installation slot | 2–3 day promise, 3–5 day contingency, two carriers, GPS, early dispatch, insurance and receiving buffer |
| FX/inflation and material-price changes | Margin erosion and quote rejection | 7–14-day quote validity, deposit before procurement, clear escalation clause, price in naira only at confirmed exchange basis |
| Fabrication/installation damage | Warranty cost and reputational loss | Local audit, test cuts, approved templates, trained installers, photos at each handover, claim protocol |
| Natural stone/tiles and Caesarstone competition | Price pressure and specification displacement | Sell total installed value, performance/application fit, design support, mock-ups and project references; do not compete only on slab price |
| Oil-sector volatility and political/security risk | Delayed projects and corporate relocation | Diversify across private luxury homes, developers, hospitality, contractors and Onne service companies |
| Channel conflict with Stone Depot or future partners | Lead leakage and discounting | Lead registration, clear territory/role rules, non-exclusive pilot, transparent transfer price and service SLA |
| Overestimating market from public luxury listings | Excess inventory and false forecast | Treat all public project pages as leads; require a named decision-maker, drawings, estimated m² and procurement timing before forecasting |

## Bottom line

Port Harcourt merits a disciplined pilot, not a speculative standalone warehouse. The most defensible path is Stone Depot-controlled supply from Lekki, a local technical/fabrication partner after audit, and direct project specification in GRA/Woji/Peter Odili/Trans-Amadi/Onne. The city’s oil-and-gas and premium-residential signals are real, but the public evidence does not justify a precise market-size claim or immediate full sub-distributor investment.
